# ICP and positioning

## Product

**Opterra AI Receptionist** — an AI practice operations platform: AI voice
receptionist, practice management dashboard, automated patient workflows, and a
patient portal. Built for alternative wellness practices — acupuncture, med
spas, float spas, IV therapy, massage, chiropractic.

## Who we are writing to

Owner-operators of wellness practices with **2–10 practitioners** who are stuck
running operations themselves and losing time, patients, and revenue as a
result.

- Already paying for at least one practice management tool
- Frustrated by missed calls, no-shows, manual scheduling, disconnected software
- Has genuine patient relationships they do not want to lose to automation
- Values calm, ease, and professionalism — not hustle, hacks, or growth tactics
- Usually the sole decision-maker; time-poor but relationship-driven

## Positioning

A premium, integrated replacement for the fragmented software stack that
wellness owners are already paying for and already frustrated by.

| Competitor | The complaint owners actually voice |
|---|---|
| Mindbody | Expensive, bloated, built for big gyms |
| Vagaro | Clunky UI, poor support |
| Zenoti | Enterprise complexity in a small-practice world |
| Mangomint | Good UI, missing AI and automation depth |
| Jane App | Canada-centric, limited for US integrations |

**The real competitor is the cobbled-together stack** — one tool for
scheduling, another for notes, another for texting, and a human receptionist
fielding calls that never get answered. Most ads should be written against that,
not against a named vendor.

## Awareness stages

| Stage | What they already believe | What the ad has to do |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-aware | "my front desk is a mess" | name the frustration precisely |
| Solution-aware | "maybe software fixes this" | show why the stack is the problem |
| Product-aware | "opterra might work" | proof, specifics, objection handling |
| Most-aware | "how much, how fast" | offer clarity and a calm CTA |
